The basic cable of IPTV

Free, ad-supported channels are everywhere: streaming services like Samsung TV Plus and Pluto TV, and broadcasters' own public streams. The community lists that collect them are long and unsorted. Open M3U is the layer on top that organizes them like a cable lineup.

  • sources

    Free streams that their owners make publicly available, merged into one playlist. Nothing that needs a login or subscription, nothing copy-protected, and no workarounds for region restrictions.

  • health

    Every stream is opened before it's listed: the playlist, its best quality, and a real video segment. Dead, copy-protected and below-720p channels don't make it. Streams limited to their own country are checked from inside it.

  • dedupe

    When the same channel is on two services, the better stream wins and the other is dropped.

  • numbers

    Groups get their own hundred, the way cable does it: local stations below 100, news at 100, sports at 200, movies at 300. Numbers stay put between builds.

  • order

    Broadcasters and big networks first in every group, and local news city by city.

  • series

    Channels that loop a single show are spotted from their schedule and shelved, A to Z, in their own group.

  • guide

    Listings for every slot, merged from several sources. Show names stay clean in the grid; episode numbers lead the description and movies carry their year.

Add it to TiviMate
  1. Open TiviMate's settings, go to Playlists, and choose Add playlist.
  2. Pick Enter URL and paste the playlist link for your country.
  3. Press Next. TiviMate reads the guide link from the playlist and loads it for you.
  4. Name it Open M3U and press Done.

In other apps (IPTV Smarters, Kodi's PVR IPTV Simple Client, Channels, Jellyfin), add it as an M3U URL. If the app asks for an EPG separately, use the guide link from your country's fold-out.

Set the playlist and guide to update daily so new channels appear and dead ones drop off.

About these streams

Open M3U doesn't host, record, relay or modify any video. The playlists are lists of links to streams that their owners publish for free, with their own ads, the same links that public community projects like iptv-org already collect. Your app plays each stream directly from its owner.

Channels behind a login, a subscription or copy protection are never included, and nothing here gets around a channel's regional restrictions. Many channels only play in their own country.

Open M3U isn't affiliated with or endorsed by any channel, network or streaming service. Channel names and logos belong to their owners. Each service's own terms of use still apply to how you watch it.

If you own or represent a channel and would rather it wasn't listed, ask here and it will be removed.

Open M3U is an open-source curator. Channel lists come from Matt Huisman's feeds, iptv-org and its database; guide data from epgshare01 and Matt's feeds; logos from tv-logos.
